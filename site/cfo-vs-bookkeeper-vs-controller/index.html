Fractional CFO vs. bookkeeper vs. controller.

Charleston, SC · Serving clients across the Southeast

Direct answer

A bookkeeper records, a controller verifies, a CFO decides.

A bookkeeper records and categorizes financial transactions. A controller checks that those records are accurate, closes the books, and produces reliable financial statements. A CFO uses that reporting to set strategy: cash planning, forecasting, pricing, and decisions about hiring, capital, and growth. The three roles are sequential and complementary, not interchangeable. Strategic Finance covers all three in one fractional CFO engagement rather than requiring separate hires for each layer.

The roles compared

What each role actually covers.

  • Bookkeeper.

    Records transactions, reconciles accounts, handles payroll entries and day-to-day data entry. Backward-looking: captures what already happened.

  • Controller.

    Owns the close process, internal controls, and accuracy of the financial statements the bookkeeper's records feed into. Makes sure the numbers are right before anyone uses them.

  • CFO.

    Forward-looking: uses clean, controlled financial statements to forecast cash and runway, evaluate pricing and margin, and support decisions about hiring, capital, and growth. Also owns lender and investor-facing reporting.

  • Strategic Finance's model.

    One accountable owner across all three layers, inside the client's own QuickBooks Online. Cleanup and controls are built into the CFO engagement rather than billed as a separate bookkeeping contract.

Frequently asked

Common questions.

What's the difference between the three roles?

A bookkeeper records and categorizes transactions. A controller verifies that those records are accurate and produces reliable financial statements. A CFO uses that reporting to set financial strategy: cash planning, forecasting, pricing, and decisions about hiring, capital, and growth. The roles build on each other rather than replace one another.

Do I need all three, or can one person cover more than one?

Most small and mid-sized businesses can't justify three full-time hires. Strategic Finance's fractional CFO model covers the full stack in one engagement: the bookkeeping foundation, the controller-level accuracy and controls, and the CFO-level strategy, all from one accountable owner.

Why not just hire a bookkeeper and skip the CFO layer?

A bookkeeper tells you what already happened. If the business needs to answer forward-looking questions, such as how much cash is available next quarter, whether a hire is affordable, or whether the numbers will survive a lender's review, bookkeeping alone doesn't answer those. That's CFO-level work.

What does Strategic Finance do differently from a typical CFO firm?

Tom Schipper owns the finance function end to end, from the bookkeeping foundation up through strategic decision support, rather than advising on top of someone else's books. All work happens inside the client's own QuickBooks Online, so the books stay the client's if the relationship ever changes.
