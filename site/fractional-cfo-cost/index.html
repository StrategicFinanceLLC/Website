Direct answer

Fractional CFO cost depends on scope, not a flat rate card.

What a fractional CFO costs depends on the condition of the books, how many locations or entities need consolidating, how much forecasting and reporting the business needs, and whether capital-raise or lender support is in scope. Strategic Finance does not publish flat pricing because the right scope for a single-location business with clean books looks nothing like the right scope for a multi-location practice heading into a capital raise. Every engagement starts with a Clarity Call to establish what's actually needed before any number is discussed.

What drives scope

The variables that set the price, not a price itself.

  • Condition of the books.

    Clean, current books take less work to build on than books that need a cleanup first. Cleanup is never billed as a separate project; it's part of the same engagement.

  • Number of locations or entities.

    A single-location business is a different scope than a multi-location healthcare practice or a multi-unit franchise needing consolidated, location-level reporting.

  • Forecasting & reporting depth.

    A monthly KPI pack is a different scope than a 13-week cash view, board reporting, and covenant tracking running in parallel.

  • Lender, investor, or transaction work.

    Diligence prep, data rooms, and capital-raise support add scope beyond ongoing monthly CFO work and are priced as part of the broader engagement.

When you need one

Signals it's time for a fractional CFO.

A fractional CFO is typically warranted once bookkeeping alone stops answering the questions a business actually has: what cash is available next quarter, whether a new hire or location is affordable, whether the numbers will hold up to a lender or investor, or whether the business is actually profitable by location, product, or service line. If the financial statements exist but nobody is using them to make decisions, that's the gap a fractional CFO closes.

Frequently asked

Common questions about cost.

What drives how much a fractional CFO costs?

Scope, not a fixed price list. The main drivers are the condition of the existing books, how many locations or entities need to be consolidated, how much forecasting and board or lender reporting is needed, and whether the engagement includes transaction or capital-raise support. Strategic Finance prices to the expected time commitment after a Clarity Call, not a flat published rate.

Is cleanup billed separately?

No. If the books need cleanup, that work is built into the CFO engagement, not billed as a separate project. The forecasting, cash planning, and reporting a CFO delivers only hold up if the underlying bookkeeping is accurate, so cleanup is treated as part of the same job.

How do I know if I need a fractional CFO instead of a bookkeeper?

A bookkeeper records what already happened. A fractional CFO is warranted when a business needs someone to translate those records into decisions: cash runway, pricing and margin, hiring plans, or lender and investor readiness. If financial statements exist but no one is using them to drive decisions, that's usually the signal.

How long does an engagement typically run?

At Strategic Finance, most engagements run for years, not months. The relationship starts with a Clarity Call and a Clarity Snapshot, and if there's a fit, it becomes an ongoing CFO Partnership that scales from monthly strategic sessions up to integrated financial leadership.

What's included?

Financial planning and analysis, cash and runway planning, finance function build-out including close process and KPI reporting, and lender, investor, and diligence readiness. Transaction and capital-raise support is also available. Bookkeeping cleanup is included where it's needed.
