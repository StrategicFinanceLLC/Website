Our mark

Four stones from the game of Go, and the board you can't see.

The crosscut

A position where both sides are cut.

Go is a strategy game played with black and white stones on a grid. Our mark shows a crosscut: four stones, set diagonally, where each player has cut the other's stones formation. Both sides have weaknesses and both have opportunities.

Looking only at those four stones, you can't tell who is ahead or what the right move is. The answer depends on everything around them: which groups are strong, which are weak, and what each side can afford to give up. The same four stones could be the start of a winning attack, a costly mistake, or a fight best left alone.

How we think

The numbers are part of the picture. Strategy means seeing the whole board.

A monthly P&L shows part of the position. It tells you what the business earned and spent. It doesn't tell you whether cash will hold up, where margins are exposed, or which opportunities deserve the investment. Those answers depend on the rest of the board.

Every stone is a commitment.

Each decision spends something you don't get back. Putting capital in one place means not putting it somewhere else.

Good tactics serve the larger strategy.

A short-term cash move can be exactly right. It still has to support where the business is going. We help owners win the exchange and keep the position.

Sometimes the best move is elsewhere.

A local fight can pull all your attention. Stepping back to look at the whole business often shows a better place to play.

That's how we approach financial leadership. We connect cash, margins, operations, and long-term priorities, so owners make decisions with the whole board in view. See how we work with clients.
