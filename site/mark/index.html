Our mark

Four stones from the game of Go, and the board you can't see.

The crosscut

A position where both sides are cut.

Go is a strategy game played with black and white stones on a grid. Our mark shows a crosscut: four stones, set diagonally, where each player has cut the other's stones apart. Both sides have weaknesses and both have chances.

Looking only at those four stones, you can't tell who is ahead or what the right move is. The answer depends on everything around them: which groups are strong, which are weak, and what each side can afford to give up.

Why Go

The numbers are part of the picture. Strategy means seeing the whole board.

A monthly P&L is a few stones. It tells you what happened at those points. It doesn't tell you what they mean for cash, for the balance sheet, for the team, or for the next two years. That's the work we do.

Every stone is a commitment.

In Go, each move spends a turn you don't get back. Capital works the same way. Putting it in one place means not putting it somewhere else.

Position beats tactics.

Good moves often pay off much later. We help owners build a position that holds up, not just win the next exchange.

Sometimes the best move is elsewhere.

A local fight can pull all your attention. Stepping back to look at the whole business often shows a better place to play.
